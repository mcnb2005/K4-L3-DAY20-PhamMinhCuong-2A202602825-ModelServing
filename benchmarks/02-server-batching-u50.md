Highest sampled value was **3.91 of 4** slots. Note this gauge is llama.cpp's *average* busy slots per decode step, so the number below is the highest average we sampled, not an instantaneous maximum batch width. A peak near 1 means
requests were served one at a time -- either the load was too light to overlap, or
they arrived too far apart. A peak approaching `--parallel` means the scheduler was
genuinely packing concurrent requests into shared decode steps.
`requests_deferred` went above zero: more requests arrived than there were slots, so some waited. That wait is the queue time in your P95.

## My observation

The highest sampled batch width was 3.91 of 4 slots (98%). It is lower than the
23.5 effective concurrency from Little's Law because effective concurrency includes
the requests waiting behind the four active slots; the metrics sampler observed up
to 46 deferred requests. I trust `n_busy_slots_per_decode` for actual batching and
Little's Law for total in-system occupancy. Together they show full slots plus a queue.
